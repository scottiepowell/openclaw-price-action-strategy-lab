## 0. Repo Core Terms

These are the minimum working terms used across this repo. Definitions stay evidence-bound and conservative.

### Current Range
The active price leg or structure price is operating inside now. Used to orient support, resistance, and target selection.

### Support
A price area where buyers may defend price. Used for bounce zones, invalidation awareness, and downside-target context.

### Resistance
A price area where sellers may defend price. Used for rejection zones, breakout confirmation, and upside-target context.

### Target
The next meaningful destination price is aiming toward. A setup should have a target before it is treated as a candidate.

### Invalidation
The level or condition that proves the setup wrong. If invalidation is unclear, the candidate stays weak or blocked.

### Room to Target
The distance between the candidate and the next target. If room is too small, the setup is usually a no-trade.

### No-Trade Filter
A condition that blocks a candidate before it becomes actionable. Examples: unclear context, target already hit, invalidation missing, or higher-timeframe conflict.

### Candidate
A supported setup concept that is still under review. Candidates are not trade instructions.

### Replay Case
A historical evidence record used to label structure, test a candidate, or find contradictions.

### Contradiction Case
A replay record that weakens, narrows, or rejects a candidate.

### Paper-Validation Candidate
A candidate that has passed conservative readiness gates well enough to be considered for paper-only testing later.

## 1. Core Market Structure

### Direction

**Definition:**  
The expected market bias: bullish/upside or bearish/downside.

**Strategy role:**  
Direction is the first layer of the strategy. It tells the trader whether to favor calls/upside plays, puts/downside plays, or wait.

**Related terms:**  
Monthly direction, weekly direction, daily direction, minute direction, bullish, bearish, current range.

---

### Bullish

**Definition:**  
A market condition where price is expected to move higher.

**Strategy role:**  
Bullish structure favors calls, long shares, upside targets, and bullish entries.

**Visual clue:**  
Higher lows, resistance breaks, bullish ranges, price holding above support, or price breaking through a key 78/reversal level.

---

### Bearish

**Definition:**  
A market condition where price is expected to move lower.

**Strategy role:**  
Bearish structure favors puts, downside targets, bearish entries, and support-break setups.

**Visual clue:**  
Lower highs, support breaks, bearish ranges, failed pushes, or price breaking below a key support/reversal level.

---

### Turn Bullish

**Definition:**  
A shift from bearish or neutral structure into upside structure.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to identify when calls/upside plays become more favorable.

**Common evidence:**  
Price breaks resistance, breaks a key 78/reversal level, holds above a prior level, or starts aiming toward the next upside target.

---

### Turn Bearish

**Definition:**  
A shift from bullish or neutral structure into downside structure.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to identify when puts/downside plays become more favorable.

**Common evidence:**  
Price breaks support, rejects from resistance, fails to hold a level, or starts aiming toward the next downside target.

---

### Swing High

**Definition:**  
A meaningful high before price pulls back or drops.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to anchor ranges, identify resistance, locate magnetic highs, and define upside targets.

---

### Swing Low

**Definition:**  
A meaningful low before price pushes upward.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to anchor ranges, identify support, locate magnetic lows, and define downside or reversal areas.

---

### Higher Low

**Definition:**  
A pullback low that forms above a prior low.

**Strategy role:**  
Often supports bullish continuation or a bullish reversal.

---

### Lower High

**Definition:**  
A rally high that forms below a prior high.

**Strategy role:**  
Often supports bearish continuation or a bearish reversal.

---

### Lower Low

**Definition:**  
A low that breaks below the prior low.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to update bearish structure and identify new downside ranges or targets.

---

### Significant Swing Point

**Definition:**  
A swing high or swing low important enough to anchor a range, target, support, or resistance level.

**Strategy role:**  
These are the major decision points in the strategy.

## 2. Timeframe Terms

### Monthly Direction

**Definition:**  
The highest-level directional bias, based on the monthly chart.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for longer-term plays and macro directional context.

---

### Weekly Direction

**Definition:**  
Intermediate directional bias based on the weekly chart.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to connect the monthly context to daily/swing execution.

---

### Daily Direction

**Definition:**  
Shorter-term directional bias based on the daily chart.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for scalps, swings, and day-to-day execution.

---

### Minute Direction

**Definition:**  
Intraday directional bias based on minute-level charts.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for precise entries, especially 1-minute bullish or bearish entries.

---

### Top-Down Analysis

**Definition:**  
The process of starting from higher timeframes and moving down to lower timeframes.

**Strategy role:**  
Prevents entries from being taken without higher-timeframe context.

**Typical flow:**  
Monthly → Weekly → Daily → Minute.

---

### Step-Down Process

**Definition:**  
A structured workflow of moving from higher timeframe structure into lower timeframe execution.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to convert big-picture direction into an actionable entry.

---

## 3. Range Terms

### Range

**Definition:**  
A measured price movement from one meaningful point to another.

**Strategy role:**  
Ranges organize price into structure. The strategy uses ranges to identify support, resistance, targets, and entries.

---

### Current Range

**Definition:**  
The active measured price leg that price is currently operating inside.

**Strategy role:**  
This is one of the core concepts of the strategy. The current range shows the trader where price is, where it may react, and where it may go next.

**Validated visual definition:**  
A current range is usually drawn from a meaningful low to high in bullish movement, or from a meaningful high to low in bearish movement.

---

### Bullish Range

**Definition:**  
A range drawn from a meaningful low to a meaningful high.

**Strategy role:**  
Used when price is moving or expected to move upward.

**Key levels inside the range:**  
50, 61.8, 78/78.6, 1.0, and 0.

---

### Bearish Range

**Definition:**  
A range drawn from a meaningful high to a meaningful low.

**Strategy role:**  
Used when price is moving or expected to move downward.

---

### Bigger Range

**Definition:**  
A larger range built from more important swing highs and swing lows.

**Strategy role:**  
Provides broader structure and higher-confidence support/resistance/target context.

---

### Smaller Range

**Definition:**  
A smaller price leg inside a bigger range.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for tighter entries, scalps, shorter swings, and refined execution.

---

### Foundation Range

**Definition:**  
The larger, clearer range built from obvious swing highs and swing lows.

**Strategy role:**  
Used as the primary structure before refining into tighter ranges.

---

### Current Movement Range

**Definition:**  
The most recent active movement range, even if it is smaller than the foundation range.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to understand what price is doing now.

---

### Tighter Range

**Definition:**  
A refined smaller range used when the broader/foundation range is too wide or unclear for execution.

**Strategy role:**  
Helps identify closer support, closer resistance, 78 reversal levels, and redefined entries.

---

### Tighter Current Range

**Definition:**  
A smaller active range based on current local price movement.

**Strategy role:**  
Used when clean swing highs/lows are not obvious or when a more precise entry is needed.

---

### Same Candle Range

**Definition:**  
A range drawn from the high and low of a single candle.

**Strategy role:**  
Used when there is no clean push, swing, or obvious range. It creates a tighter decision area for entries.

---

### Candle Range

**Definition:**  
The high-to-low range of a candle.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to define same-candle ranges, local entries, support/resistance, and candle-based levels.

---

## 4. Fib and Range-Level Terms

### Fib Tool

**Definition:**  
The measurement tool used to divide a price range into important levels.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to identify zones such as 50, 61.8, 78/78.6, 1.0, and 0.

**Important note:**  
The course later teaches how to find similar levels without the fib by reading candles, orders, and volume.

---

### 50 Level

**Definition:**  
The halfway point of the range.

**Strategy role:**  
Part of the internal decision zone where price may react.

---

### 61.8 Level

**Definition:**  
A key internal range level near the 50 level.

**Strategy role:**  
Part of the sweet spot where price may pull back and react.

---

### 50/61 Sweet Spot

**Definition:**  
The area between the 50 and 61.8 levels.

**Strategy role:**  
A reaction zone where price may show buying or selling pressure before continuing.

---

### 78 / 78.6 Level

**Definition:**  
A key confirmation or reversal level in the strategy.

**Strategy role:**  
When price breaks or closes beyond this level, it may signal that price wants to continue toward the next target, often the 1.0 level.

---

### Reversal Level

**Definition:**  
A level that, once broken, suggests price may reverse or continue toward the next target.

**Strategy role:**  
Often associated with the 78/78.6 level.

---

### 1.0 Level

**Definition:**  
A major endpoint of the measured range.

**Strategy role:**  
Frequently used as a target.

---

### Zero Level

**Definition:**  
The opposite endpoint of the measured range.

**Strategy role:**  
Used as a target or range boundary.

---

### 1.618 / 2.618 Extension

**Definition:**  
Extended target levels beyond the normal range.

**Strategy role:**  
Used when price breaks beyond the standard 1.0 range target and continues.

---

## 5. Support and Resistance

### Support

**Definition:**  
A price area where buying pressure may appear.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for bounce zones, bullish entries, invalidation areas, and downside targets.

---

### Resistance

**Definition:**  
A price area where selling pressure may appear.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for rejection zones, bullish breakout confirmation, bearish entries, and upside targets.

---

### Bigger Support

**Definition:**  
Higher-context support from a larger range, larger timeframe, or major candle/order zone.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for stronger bounce/reversal expectations.

---

### Bigger Resistance

**Definition:**  
Higher-context resistance from a larger range, larger timeframe, or major candle/order zone.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for stronger rejection or breakout decisions.

---

### Smaller Support

**Definition:**  
A local support level inside a smaller range.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for tighter entries, scalps, and intraday decision-making.

---

### Smaller Resistance

**Definition:**  
A local resistance level inside a smaller range.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to judge whether price can continue upward toward the next high or larger resistance.

---

### Current Support

**Definition:**  
The support level created by the active current range.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to judge whether price is holding the current structure.

---

### Current Resistance

**Definition:**  
The resistance level created by the active current range.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to judge whether price can break upward or reject.

---

### Support Area

**Definition:**  
A zone where price may find buyers, rather than a single exact line.

**Strategy role:**  
Important because the course often treats candle bodies and order zones as areas.

---

### Resistance Area

**Definition:**  
A zone where price may find sellers, rather than a single exact line.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for rejection, breakout, and target decisions.

---

### Resistance Becomes Support

**Definition:**  
A level that first rejects price, then after price breaks above it, later supports price on a retest.

**Strategy role:**  
Used as confirmation that price may continue upward.

---

### Support Becomes Resistance

**Definition:**  
A level that first supports price, then after price breaks below it, later rejects price on a retest.

**Strategy role:**  
Used as confirmation that price may continue downward.

---

## 6. No-Fib Method

### No-Fib Method

**Definition:**  
Finding levels from candle structure, candle bodies, swing points, and order/volume interpretation instead of using the fib tool.

**Strategy role:**  
This is the mature price-action version of the range method.

**Core idea:**  
The fib teaches where important levels tend to appear. The no-fib method finds those levels directly from candles.

---

### No-Fib Support

**Definition:**  
Support found from candle/order structure without using the fib.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to identify where buyers may defend price.

---

### No-Fib Resistance

**Definition:**  
Resistance found from candle/order structure without using the fib.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to identify where price may reject or what level price must break to continue upward.

---

### Bigger Support Without Fib

**Definition:**  
A higher-context support level found from major prior candle/order zones.

**Strategy role:**  
Used as a larger support or bounce zone.

---

### Bigger Resistance Without Fib

**Definition:**  
A higher-context resistance level found from major prior candle/order zones near swing highs or rejection zones.

**Strategy role:**  
Used as a larger resistance or breakout decision area.

---

### Smaller Resistance Without Fib

**Definition:**  
A local resistance level identified from candle structure rather than the fib tool.

**Preferred method:**  
Find the last meaningful green candle before price turns down or pauses, then place resistance near the body/middle of that candle.

**Strategy role:**  
If price breaks this smaller resistance, the next resistance or prior high becomes the next upside target.

---

### Last Green Candle Resistance

**Definition:**  
The last meaningful green candle before a drop or pullback becomes the local resistance reference.

**Strategy role:**  
Represents prior buying orders/positive volume that price must overcome to continue upward.

---

### Middle-of-Candle Resistance

**Definition:**  
A resistance level placed through the body or middle of the selected candle rather than the wick.

**Strategy role:**  
The course treats the body/middle as a better representation of meaningful orders and volume.

---

### Smaller Resistance Ladder

**Definition:**  
A sequence of local resistance levels built from prior green candles, candle bodies, swing highs, or order zones.

**Strategy role:**  
As price breaks one resistance, the next resistance becomes the next upside objective.

---

### No-Fib Support Ladder

**Definition:**  
A sequence of support levels below price, built from candle/order zones.

**Strategy role:**  
Used as downside targets or bounce zones.

---

### No-Fib Resistance Ladder

**Definition:**  
A sequence of resistance levels above price, built from candle/order zones.

**Strategy role:**  
Used as upside targets or rejection zones.

---

## 7. Candle, Orders, and Volume

### Candlestick

**Definition:**  
The basic unit of price action.

**Strategy role:**  
The course treats candles as visual evidence of orders and volume.

---

### Green Candle

**Definition:**  
A candle where price closes higher than it opened.

**Course interpretation:**  
Represents positive orders, buying volume, or buying pressure.

---

### Red Candle

**Definition:**  
A candle where price closes lower than it opened.

**Course interpretation:**  
Represents selling orders, selling volume, or selling pressure.

---

### Candle Body

**Definition:**  
The open-to-close part of the candle.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to identify where meaningful orders/volume may be concentrated.

---

### Wick

**Definition:**  
The high or low extension beyond the candle body.

**Strategy role:**  
Can show rejection or extreme price movement, but the course often emphasizes the body/middle for order levels.

---

### Middle of Candle

**Definition:**  
The approximate center/body area of a meaningful candle.

**Strategy role:**  
Often used to place no-fib support or resistance.

---

### Candle-Derived Level

**Definition:**  
A support or resistance level drawn from a meaningful candle body, middle, or order zone.

**Strategy role:**  
Used in the no-fib method.

---

### Orders

**Definition:**  
The course’s term for buying or selling interest represented by candles, highs, lows, and levels.

**Strategy role:**  
Orders explain why price may move toward, reject from, or break through certain levels.

---

### Positive Orders

**Definition:**  
Buying-side orders represented by green candles.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to locate resistance that price must break through.

---

### Selling Orders

**Definition:**  
Selling-side orders represented by red candles.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to locate support or downside pressure zones.

---

### Volume

**Definition:**  
Participation or order flow represented by candle behavior.

**Course-specific note:**  
The instructor does not rely on a separate volume indicator. Volume is read through the candle print itself.

---

### Positive Volume

**Definition:**  
Buying volume represented by green candles.

---

### Selling Volume

**Definition:**  
Selling volume represented by red candles.

---

### Buying Pressure

**Definition:**  
Evidence that buyers are defending or pushing price upward.

**Strategy role:**  
Used around support, sweet spots, and bullish entries.

---

### Selling Pressure

**Definition:**  
Evidence that sellers are defending or pushing price downward.

**Strategy role:**  
Used around resistance, failed pushes, and bearish entries.

---

### No Indicators

**Definition:**  
The course’s approach of reading price through candles, ranges, orders, and structure rather than relying on external indicators.

**Strategy role:**  
Supports the no-fib/candle-reading method.

---

## 8. Targets

### Target

**Definition:**  
The expected destination price is aiming for.

**Strategy role:**  
Targets are used to plan exits, avoid overtrading, and define whether a play is worth taking.

---

### Major Target

**Definition:**  
A larger target from monthly, weekly, or major range structure.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to guide broad direction.

---

### Monthly Target

**Definition:**  
A higher-timeframe target derived from the monthly chart or monthly range.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for macro directional bias and longer plays.

---

### Weekly Target

**Definition:**  
A target derived from weekly structure.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for intermediate swing planning.

---

### Daily Target

**Definition:**  
A target derived from daily structure.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for shorter-term plays, daily trades, and scalps.

---

### Minute Target

**Definition:**  
An intraday target derived from minute-level structure.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for precise scalp entries and exits.

---

### Upside Target

**Definition:**  
A target above current price.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for calls, bullish entries, and resistance-break plays.

---

### Downside Target

**Definition:**  
A target below current price.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for puts, bearish entries, and support-break plays.

---

### Closest Target

**Definition:**  
The nearest logical destination after a break or entry.

**Strategy role:**  
Important for scalps and redefined entries because it defines realistic profit potential.

---

### Previous High Target

**Definition:**  
A prior high that price may aim for.

**Strategy role:**  
Often treated as a magnetic high because orders or stops may be located there.

---

### Previous Low Target

**Definition:**  
A prior low that price may aim for.

**Strategy role:**  
Often treated as a downside magnetic target.

---

### New Target

**Definition:**  
An updated target after price creates a new high, new low, or new range.

**Strategy role:**  
Keeps the analysis current as price develops.

---

### Target Hit

**Definition:**  
Price reaches the expected destination.

**Strategy role:**  
Signals that the planned trade idea has done its job.

---

### Play Over

**Definition:**  
The trade idea is complete because price reached the target.

**Strategy role:**  
Prevents forcing more trades after the objective is met.

---

Used to identify where price may want to go next.

---

---

A prior high that price may aim for after breaking resistance.

**Strategy role:**  
Often used as an upside target.

---

### Magnetic Low

**Strategy role:**  
Often used as a downside target.

---

### Orders at Highs

### Orders at Lows

---

### Take Orders

### Attack Highs

### Attack Lows

## 10. Entry and Execution

### Entry

**Definition:**  
The location or trigger where the trader takes the trade.

**Strategy role:**  
Entry comes after direction, range, target, and level context are understood.

---

### Redefined Entry

**Definition:**  
A more precise entry created by narrowing a broad range into a tighter range.

**Strategy role:**  
Improves risk/reward and avoids entering from an overly wide structure.

---

### Tighter Entry

**Definition:**  
An entry based on a tighter range, candle range, or smaller support/resistance level.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for precision, especially on lower timeframes.

---

### Bullish Entry

**Definition:**  
An entry expecting price to move upward.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for calls, long shares, or bullish continuation plays.

---

### Bearish Entry

**Definition:**  
An entry expecting price to move downward.

**Strategy role:**  
Used for puts or bearish continuation plays.

---

### Bullish Entry Without Fib

**Definition:**  
A long/call-side entry found from candle/order structure instead of a fib overlay.

**Strategy role:**  
The trader marks meaningful no-fib levels, then waits for price to build above them, break resistance, or confirm continuation on the 1-minute chart.

---

### Bearish Entry Without Fib

**Definition:**  
A short/put-side entry found from candle/order structure instead of a fib overlay.

**Strategy role:**  
The trader marks meaningful no-fib levels, then waits for price to lose support, reject from resistance, or confirm downside continuation on the 1-minute chart.

---

### One-Minute Entry

**Definition:**  
Precise execution on the 1-minute chart after higher-timeframe context is established.

**Strategy role:**  
Used to time entries more accurately.

---

### One-Minute Bullish Entry

**Definition:**  
A 1-minute entry where price builds a base, breaks upward, holds structure, and moves toward the next upside target.

---

### One-Minute Bearish Entry

**Definition:**  
A 1-minute entry where price breaks down, rejects, or retests a level before continuing lower.

---

### Bullish Base

**Definition:**  
A small consolidation after an initial push up.

**Strategy role:**  
Can provide a cleaner bullish entry than chasing the first candle.

---

### Break-and-Hold

**Definition:**  
Price breaks above a level and holds above it instead of immediately failing.

**Strategy role:**  
Used as bullish confirmation.

---

### Breakdown Level

**Definition:**  
A support or trigger level that price must lose before a bearish entry becomes valid.

---

### Retest After Breakdown

**Definition:**  
After price breaks below support, it retests that level from below before continuing lower.

**Strategy role:**  
Can provide a cleaner bearish entry.

---

### Bearish Continuation Entry

**Definition:**  
An entry taken after price has already confirmed downside structure through a breakdown, rejection, or retest.

---

### Enter After Break

**Definition:**  
Waiting for price to break a key level before entering.

**Strategy role:**  
Avoids guessing before confirmation.

---

### Enter Toward Target

**Definition:**  
Entering only when there is enough room between the entry level and the next target.

**Strategy role:**  
Protects risk/reward.

---

### Close Above Level

**Definition:**  
A candle closes above a marked level.

**Strategy role:**  
Used as bullish confirmation.

---

### Close Below Level

**Definition:**  
A candle closes below a marked level.

**Strategy role:**  
Used as bearish confirmation.

---

### Don’t Chase First Candle

**Definition:**  
Avoid entering simply because a large candle appears.

**Strategy role:**  
Wait for structure, base, retest, or confirmation.

---

## 12. Process Terms

### Step-by-Step Strategy

**Definition:**  
The repeatable workflow for finding trades.

**Likely sequence:**  
1. Identify direction.  
2. Identify current range.  
3. Identify support/resistance.  
4. Identify target.  
5. Refine entry.  
6. Execute only if there is room to target.

---

### Foundation First

**Definition:**  
Start with the clearest swing highs/lows and foundation ranges before refining.

---

### Refine Only When Needed

**Definition:**  
Use tighter ranges or same-candle ranges when the broad structure is too unclear or too wide.

---

### Wait for Confirmation

**Definition:**  
Avoid entering before price breaks, closes, holds, or rejects at a meaningful level.

---

### Don’t Trade All Day

**Definition:**  
Once price reaches the intended target, avoid forcing more trades unless a new setup forms.

---

### Account Management

**Definition:**  
Managing risk, position size, and trade frequency.

**Strategy role:**  
Especially important for scalping.

Watch for price to react around the 50/61 sweet spot.

### Rule 5 — The 78/78.6 level is a major decision level

A break or close beyond the 78/78.6 level may signal continuation toward the next target.

---

### Rule 6 — The 1.0 level is a major target

Price often aims for the 1.0 endpoint once the range confirms.

---

### Rule 7 — No-fib levels come from candles

Without the fib, use candle bodies, candle middles, swing highs/lows, and order zones.

---

### Rule 8 — Last green candle can define resistance

For smaller resistance, use the last meaningful green candle before the drop or pause.

---

### Rule 9 — Candle body matters more than exact wick in many no-fib examples

The body/middle often represents the most meaningful orders and volume.

---

### Rule 10 — Break one resistance, look to the next

Resistance can form a ladder: once one level breaks, the next prior high or resistance becomes the target.

---

### Rule 11 — Break one support, look to the next

Support can form a ladder: once one support breaks, the next lower support or prior low becomes the target.

---

### Rule 12 — Price moves toward orders

Prior highs and lows can act as magnetic targets because orders or stops may sit there.

---

### Rule 13 — Do not chase the first candle

Wait for base, break, retest, or confirmation.

---

### Rule 14 — Entry must have room to target

Do not enter if the nearest target is too close to justify the trade.

---

### Rule 15 — Once the target is hit, the play is complete

Avoid forcing new trades unless a fresh setup forms.
